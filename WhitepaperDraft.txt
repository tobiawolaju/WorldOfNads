1. WHAT IS WORLD OF NADS
Twenty players drop into an arena. One chicken spawns somewhere on the map. Hold it when the clock hits zero and the prize pool hits your wallet -- settled onchain.
That is the entire game. Simple enough to understand in ten seconds. Deep enough that no two matches play the same way.
World of Nads is a browser-based competitive Tag Royale. It runs at 60 frames per second on desktop and mobile Chrome. No download, no app store, no seed phrase required on the free tier. A player in Lagos, London, or Los Angeles opens a link and is in a match within fifteen seconds.
Underneath is a hybrid architecture: smart contracts handle prize settlement and skin ownership. A WebSocket game server handles real-time physics and player sync -- because no blockchain today can settle individual game ticks at sub-20ms latency. This two-layer design is intentional and transparent. Off-chain handles what blockchains cannot. On-chain handles what centralized servers cannot.
World of Nads is built for two audiences: Web3 natives who want real ownership and instant payouts, and Web2 gamers who just want something fun to play.
The game deploys on Monad Testnet today with four live smart contracts. The architecture supports both Monad and Solana -- the two chains with sub-cent gas costs that make per-match micro-viable. Section 5 covers the chain-specific decisions.

2. THE PROBLEM WITH WEB3 GAMING
Web3 gaming has raised billions of dollars and retained almost nobody.
The charts tell the same story every time. A new game launches. Token price rises. Players flood in chasing yield. Token price falls. Players leave overnight. The cycle repeats with a different name and a different token and the same outcome.
This is not bad luck. It is bad architecture. The industry built financial instruments and called them games.


Token Dependency: When a game's retention depends on its token price, it has no retention. It has speculation. The moment the financial incentive weakens -- and it always weakens -- the player has no reason to stay. There is no fun to return to because fun was never the point. The game was always a yield farm wearing a character skin.

Pay to Win: Competitive integrity is the foundation of every game worth playing. Remove it and you do not have a game. You have a storefront where the wealthiest wallet wins and calls it skill. No skilled player stays in a game where their effort is overridden by someone else's credit card. No new player joins a game where losing is guaranteed until they spend enough to compete.

The Bot Plague: Web3 projects spend millions on airdrop campaigns designed to acquire real users. The majority of recipients are automated scripts. A bot cannot feel excitement. A bot cannot tell a friend about a game. A bot cannot become a loyal player. It can only drain the prize pool and leave. The industry has accepted this as normal. It is not normal. It is a waste of capital.

Fake Onchain Games: Most games that call themselves onchain are not onchain. The smart contract handles the token. The server handles everything else. The game state lives in a database the developer controls. If the developer disappears tomorrow the game disappears with them. If the server goes down the game goes down. Players are told they own their assets. They own a record of a transaction. The asset itself lives somewhere else and can be changed or destroyed.

3. THE SOLUTION -- PLAY AND OWN
Those four problems share a single root cause. The industry kept asking how to add blockchain to games instead of asking how to build games that blockchain makes fundamentally better. World of Nads starts from the second question.
World of Nads is built on four principles:


Fun First: The game has to be worth playing without any financial incentive attached. This is not a philosophical position. It is a survival strategy. World of Nads was designed to pass one test before anything else: would a Web2 gamer with no wallet and no interest in crypto play this for twenty minutes and come back tomorrow? The chicken mechanic is chaotic, readable, and immediately competitive. Twenty players, one objective, no complicated ruleset. The tension of holding the chicken while nineteen people chase you does not require understanding blockchain to feel real. It requires reflexes and decisions. That feeling is the product.

Skill Based Equality: Nothing purchasable in World of Nads affects gameplay. A player with the rarest skin competes on exactly the same terms as a player with the default skin. Cosmetics are the only monetisation layer. They change how you look. They do not change how you perform.

Instant Trustless Payouts: When a match ends the prize pool moves. The smart contract settles the match and funds transfer to the winner's wallet. Entry fees lock into the contract on deposit and release on settlement. No human holds the prize money at any point.

Own What You Earn: Every skin in World of Nads is a limited supply onchain asset. When you own a skin you own it the way you own anything onchain -- completely, verifiably, permanently. You can equip it, transfer it, list it for sale, or hold it as supply runs out. You cannot do these things in any Web2 game.

An Honest Note On V1
World of Nads V1 uses a centralized relay server for real-time game physics and player synchronisation. This is a deliberate architectural choice.
Real-time multiplayer at 60 frames per second requires sub-20ms latency. No blockchain can settle individual physics ticks at that speed. The relay handles what blockchains cannot -- moment to moment game state. The smart contract handles what centralized servers cannot -- trustless custody and settlement of prize funds.
WONs2 introduces an open source relay node that anyone can deploy, a contract registry where nodes publish their WebSocket URLs, and a peer verification system. If the team disappears, the community spins up new nodes. The game continues. Section 11 covers the full roadmap.

4. GAMEPLAY -- HOW IT WORKS

The Arena & The Near-Miss Loop: Twenty players drop into an open desert arena from a bus. The map is large enough for strategic positioning and small enough that standing still is never safe. Terrain undulations create natural corridors. Scattered props -- shipping containers, wooden platforms, crates, abandoned structures -- create cover clusters where the most intense possession fights happen.
A colored zone closes in throughout the match, compressing the playable space until the final thirty seconds force every remaining player into a small circle. The zone always collapses toward the center of the map. Map knowledge is skill expression.
Matches last 3-5 minutes.

The "Almost Winning" XP System: Retention is built on the WONsXP system. Even if you don't win the prize pool, every second of possession and every match completed earns you XP. XP gates access to high-tier skins and is permanently recorded onchain.

The Chicken: One chicken spawns somewhere on the map at match start. Five possible spawn locations rotate unpredictably. Holding the chicken does three things simultaneously. First, it drains your stamina -- the longer you hold, the slower you become, preventing camping. Second, it earns you XP that streams toward your onchain account as a soulbound token. Third, the player holding the chicken when the timer reaches zero wins the prize pool.

The Loot Box: Separate from the chicken, a loot box drops on the map mid-match with its own value. It can be stolen by any player who reaches it. Every time the loot box changes ownership it costs gas, deducted from the box's value. On Monad that gas cost is approximately $0.001 per transfer. On Solana approximately $0.00025. The box survives hundreds of steals while shrinking visibly. The HUD shows the loot box's current value live, creating a real-time economic decision: fight now and drain the box, or wait and steal at the right moment.

The HUD -- Watching Money Move
During a match the heads-up display shows:
Prize Pool: Current value of the chicken. What the match winner receives.
Your Earnings: XP accumulated from holding time. Ticks up every second. Gates high-tier skins.
Loot Box: Current value of the loot box. Decreases with each steal.
Your Wallet: Total XP earned this match so far.

Match Format
Players per match: 20
Match duration: 3-5 minutes
Platform: Browser, no download
Device support: Mobile and desktop
Entry: Free tier (playable with mock/sponsor matches) / Paid tier (sponsored real-MON matches)
Prize settlement: Onchain, triggered by backend after match end
Skin sync: Equipped skin loads in match, visible to all players

Free To Play Layer
World of Nads has a free entry tier where players can play without spending real MON. Sponsors fund prize pools for promotion. The free tier exists because Web2 gamers arrive curious and cautious. When they are hooked, conversion to paid matches is a single step. During V1 beta, all prize pools are intentionally limited.

5. CHAIN MECHANICS -- MONAD AND SOLANA
Most projects that describe themselves as blockchain games use the chain for one thing: token transfers. The game runs on a Web2 server. The blockchain processes payments. World of Nads uses the chain for settlement, skin ownership, match verification, and micro-reward distribution.

Why Low Gas Cost Is A Game Design Variable
Every blockchain transaction costs gas. On most L1s that cost is high enough that per-action micro-transactions are uneconomical. On Monad and Solana the cost per transaction is fractions of a cent -- $0.001 on Monad, ~$0.00025 on Solana. This makes per-second rewards, per-steal deductions, and instant settlement viable as game mechanics.

Monad: The Current Deployment
World of Nads is deployed on Monad Testnet with four live smart contracts: WONsMatchEngine (match creation, settlement, ERC-1155 winner/participation NFTs), WONsLootBox (prize pool with MON streaming and steal mechanics), WONsXP (soulbound ERC-20 XP), and WONsSkins (ERC-1155 skin marketplace with XP gating).
Mechanic 1 -- The Loot Box Value Destruction: A loot box drops mid-match with a value funded by the sponsor. Every steal is tracked. On Monad the gas cost per steal is ~$0.001, deducted from the box value. With a starting value of 20 MON, the box can survive thousands of steals. This creates a real economic decision: fight aggressively and drain the box, or wait and steal at the right moment.

Mechanic 2 -- XP Per Second (Soulbound Rewards): XP is tracked server-side per second of chicken possession and minted as soulbound tokens at match end. On Monad the gas cost to mint XP for all participants is approximately $0.72 per match. This is economically viable. On Ethereum the same operation would cost over $10,000.

Mechanic 3 -- Prize Settlement: Entry fees lock into the match contract on match creation. When the match ends, the backend (trusted authority) calls settleMatch on the contract. The contract verifies the signature, pays the winner, and mints participation NFTs. No human handles the funds at any point.

Solana: The Second Deployment Target
Solana offers even lower per-transaction fees (~$0.00025) and the largest gaming ecosystem in crypto. The game's architecture is chain-agnostic at the relay and frontend layer. The frontend already has full Solana wallet support via Privy. Porting settlement logic to Solana requires Solana-native smart contracts (Anchor/Sealevel) for match settlement, loot box mechanics, and skin management. The game server and client remain identical; only the on-chain settlement layer changes.
Solana's high throughput and sub-second finality make it an ideal second chain for World of Nads. The per-steal loot box cost is even lower, and the ecosystem has proven gaming infrastructure (marketplaces, wallets, stablecoins).

The Math Summary
On Ethereum: Loot box dies after ~7 steals. Micro-reward gas per match: $10,800. Viable as game mechanic: No.
On Monad: Loot box survives 20,000+ steals. Micro-reward gas per match: $0.72. Steal cost per action: $0.001. Viable: Yes.
On Solana: Loot box survives 80,000+ steals. Micro-reward gas per match: ~$0.18. Steal cost per action: ~$0.00025. Viable: Yes.
The difference between $10,800 and $0.72 (or $0.18 on Solana) is not a technical footnote. It is the difference between a mechanic that exists and one that cannot.
World of Nads is on Monad because Monad makes the game possible. It targets Solana because Solana makes it even cheaper and connects the largest gaming audience in crypto.

5.5 Why Not Simulate Off-Chain?
A common critique is: "Why not simulate the loot box decay on a centralized server and just settle the final balance at match end?"
World of Nads uses onchain settlement for three reasons:

1. Verifiable Truth. An off-chain loot box value is a number in a database the developer controls. An onchain loot box is a smart contract. Its value is immutable record.

2. Ecosystem Composability. By settling onchain, the game becomes composable infrastructure. Other smart contracts -- betting protocols, analytics dashboards, reward aggregators -- can interact with match results while the game is live.

3. Continuous Security. Settling only at match end creates a lump-sum risk. If the server crashes in the final ten seconds, the player loses everything. By committing match results and micro-rewards onchain, what you earn is yours regardless of server availability.

The Gas Sustainability Model
Who pays for onchain transactions in a free-to-play game? The loot box carries its own gas. Every steal deducts gas from the box's pool. XP minting is subsidized by the protocol or sponsor fees in sponsored matches.
Match costs:
- XP commits: ~$0.72/match on Monad, ~$0.18/match on Solana (paid from sponsor or protocol)
- Loot box steals: deducted from loot box pool (self-funding)
- Match settlement: one transaction
This ensures the game remains gas-free for the end user while the economy sustains itself.

7. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
World of Nads uses a three-layer architecture that assigns each responsibility to the layer best suited to handle it.

LAYER 1: GAME CLIENT (GODOT + WASM)
The game client runs entirely in a browser using the Godot Engine exported to WebAssembly.
Rendering: Godot engine, 60fps desktop target, 45fps mobile target
Export: WebAssembly with WASM SIMD and threaded builds for ~20-30% performance gain over baseline
Textures: Single color palette atlas, under 500KB, WebP format
Models: GLB format, under 50KB per model, LOD switching at distance
Mobile detection: UA string on load, viewport scaling 0.75 on mobile, shadow resolution 512, zero MSAA
Asset loading: All assets preloaded during server wake countdown, zero mid-match stutter
Chunk system: The world is divided into proximity-based chunks loaded based on player location. Full detail at 0-30m, LOD 2 meshes at 30-80m, billboard sprites at 80-150m, location data only beyond 150m.

LAYER 2: AUTHORITATIVE GAME SERVER (NODE.JS + WEBSOCKET)
The relay is a Node.js WebSocket server hosted on Google Cloud Run / Render. It is the single source of truth for live match state.
Player input arrives: <1ms processing
State broadcast: Every 50ms (20Hz)
Physics tick: 20Hz (decoupled from render)
Chicken possession update: Real-time, server-authoritative
Stamina calculation: Server-authoritative. Client cannot fake stamina.
Zone shrink update: Broadcast every second
Match timer: Server-authoritative. Client display only.
The server performs deterministic reconciliation -- checking player velocity and chicken proximity to prevent teleporting or speed-hacking. State compression broadcasts only changed data (delta compression) to minimize packet size for mobile browsers on low-bandwidth connections.
At match end the relay signs the result (winner address, match ID, final timestamp) with a private key. This signed result is submitted to the settlement contract. The contract verifies the signature before releasing funds.
Cloud Run / Render Scaling: Backend instances scale automatically with player count. Each match instance is isolated. Costs scale with usage.
- 1-100 concurrent matches: ~$50/month
- 100-500 concurrent matches: ~$150/month
- 500-2,000 concurrent matches: ~$400/month

LAYER 3: ON-CHAIN SETTLEMENT (MONAD / SOLANA)
The settlement layer lives onchain. On Monad today, four contracts handle the complete match lifecycle:

WONsMatchEngine -- Creates and settles sponsored matches. Sponsor calls createSponsoredMatch which locks the prize pool. The backend calls settleMatch with the winner address and participant list. The contract pays the winner (80%), distributes to loot box (20%), and mints ERC-1155 winner/participation NFTs.
WONsLootBox -- Holds the loot box prize pool. Supports fundPool, streamMON (award MON to chicken holders), batchStream, steal (redirect stream on chicken steal, deducts 0.001 MON gas), settle (distribute remaining to final holder), drainPool.
WONsXP -- Soulbound ERC-20 XP token. Only the backend (trusted minter) can mint XP. Non-transferable by design. XP gates skin purchases.
WONsSkins -- ERC-1155 skin marketplace. Create skin (admin), mint skin (player, XP + MON gated), burn skins (common tier only, 2 minimum, triggers rare mint from reserve).

On Solana (planned), equivalent Anchor programs will handle the same logic: a match program, loot box program, XP token (SPL with transfer restrictions), and skin collection (Compressed NFTs or Metaplex).

Bridge Architecture (WONs2)
The bridge separates real funds from game operations. Not yet deployed -- planned for WONs2.
Player deposits real MON/SOL into the bridge contract. Testnet tokens credited to in-game balance. All game contract interactions happen on testnet. Real funds held in bridge are protected. Exploit drains testnet tokens, not real funds.

CLIENT ARCHITECTURE
Frontend Shell: React + TypeScript (Vite) with Privy wallet authentication. Supports both EVM and Solana wallets. Connects to Firebase for match/user data.
Godot Client: WebAssembly export embedded in the React app. Receives username, skin, and match context. Connects to the WebSocket game server.
Communication flow: Player opens browser -> React app creates/joins match via Firebase -> Godot client loads -> WebSocket connects to game server -> match plays out -> result submitted onchain -> React app shows result.

DATA STORES
Firebase RTDB: Player data, match metadata, reward records, wallet addresses. Real-time sync for lobby and match status.
PostgreSQL: Analytics pipeline -- event tracking, aggregation, admin dashboards.
In-memory server state: Connected players, chicken holder and position, match timer, recent event history (last 200). This is the authoritative state for active rounds.

WONs2 -- THE DECENTRALISATION ARCHITECTURE
WONs2 introduces a fully decentralized relay network. The architecture is designed. Implementation is the second funded milestone.
Anyone clones the open source relay -> relay hashes its own code on startup -> hash compared to canonical hash stored in onchain contract -> mismatch = relay refuses to start -> match = relay connects to peer nodes -> peer consensus on code integrity -> relay registers WebSocket URL in contract -> frontend reads relay list from contract -> player connects to first live relay -> if team disappears, community spins up new relays, game continues.
The frontend follows the same pattern: IPFS deployment with content hash committed to an onchain contract. Any gateway serving the correct content hash is valid. The smart contract is the only permanent component. Everything else can be replaced, forked, or redeployed.

8. THE SKIN ECONOMY
Every skin in World of Nads is a limited supply onchain asset. Not a license to display an image. Not a database entry a company controls. An asset recorded onchain that belongs to the wallet that holds it -- completely, verifiably, permanently.
Skins are gated by XP. You cannot buy a Seraphim with MON alone -- you must have earned enough XP by holding the chicken across matches. XP is soulbound and non-transferable. It cannot be bought or botted. It must be earned in-game. This replaces pay-to-win with play-to-unlock.

Ownership That Means Something
When you buy a skin in Fortnite you receive permission to display that skin while Fortnite exists, while your account remains in good standing, and while Epic Games chooses to honor that permission. When you own a skin in World of Nads the ownership record lives onchain. No team decision removes it. No server shutdown deletes it. The asset is yours because the chain says so.

The Economic Model
World of Nads V1 ships with 8 founding skins across four rarity tiers -- Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary. Every skin operates on three rules enforced by the smart contract:
Supply is fixed at deployment. Once a skin's mint cap is reached it cannot be increased.
Every transfer costs gas and generates royalties. The smart contract deducts a creator royalty and a protocol fee automatically on every peer-to-peer transaction.
Skins are designed with cultural tension. Paired opposites, community archetypes, limited-edition collaborations.

The Secondary Market
When a skin's primary supply is exhausted it transitions to a peer-to-peer listing view. Three mechanisms are available:
Fixed Price: Seller sets price. Buyer clicks buy. Instant transfer.
Open Bid: Buyer submits offer. Seller accepts any bid. Funds held in escrow.
Timed Auction: Countdown timer. Highest bidder at expiry wins. Contract executes automatically.
Players can also make unsolicited offers on any skin regardless of listing status.

Revenue Split
Every skin transaction splits automatically through the smart contract.
Primary sale: 10% protocol fee to treasury, 90% to team wallet.
Secondary sale: 5% creator royalty to founder wallet (permanent, immutable), 5% protocol fee to treasury, 90% to current holder.
The creator royalty is hardcoded into the contract at deployment. It pays on every secondary transaction for as long as the contract exists.

The Burn Mechanic
Two common skins can be burned -- permanently destroyed -- in exchange for one random rare or epic skin from a fixed reserve pool. Burned skins are removed from circulation permanently. Net supply decreases.

An Expanding Roster
V1 is the foundation. The skin roster grows through partner collaborations (Web3 projects, NFT collections commission skins through the IP licensing registry), seasonal drops (limited-time skins with smallest supply caps, never return after their window closes), and DAO governance (in WONs2, community proposes and votes on new skin series).

What Fortnite Cannot Do
Fortnite has more players, more skins, more revenue. World of Nads is not competing with Fortnite on scale. It is offering something Fortnite structurally cannot offer: actual ownership of the asset you paid for, in a market that never closes, on a chain that never goes down.

9. BUSINESS MODEL
World of Nads generates revenue from three independent streams.

The Design Principle
Every revenue mechanism passes one test: does it make the game better or worse for the player who does not spend money? A player who never spends a single MON beyond their entry fee experiences the full competitive game. They can win matches, top the leaderboard, earn XP, and acquire skins through the burn mechanic. The game does not punish non-spenders.

Stream 1 -- B2C Player Revenue
Battle Pass: Monthly subscription at $1-$2 giving access to premium match tiers with larger prize pools and exclusive seasonal cosmetics. No gameplay advantage.
Month 1: 200 subscribers = $200-$400
Month 2: 500 subscribers = $500-$1,000
Month 3: 1,000 subscribers = $1,000-$2,000
Month 4: 2,000 subscribers = $2,000-$4,000

Skin Sales: Primary skin sales generate one-time revenue at launch. Launch week projection (realistic 20% conversion): $7,000-$10,000.

Premium Match Tickets: Beyond the battle pass, high-stakes match tickets at $5-$10 per entry. Protocol takes 10%.
3 premium matches per day x $200 prize pool x 10% fee = $60/day = $1,800/month.

Stream 2 -- B2B Sponsor Revenue
Web3 projects spend significant capital on community activation and user acquisition. Most goes to airdrop campaigns that reach bots. World of Nads offers a branded tournament where real players compete for prizes the sponsor funds, with every participant wallet recorded onchain.
Pricing: Pilot match $200-$500, Standard package $500-$1,000, Premium package $1,000-$3,000.
4-month pipeline total: $5,900-$13,000.

Stream 3 -- Secondary Market Royalties
Every peer-to-peer skin transfer pays a 5% creator royalty. Conservative month 2-4 estimates: $40/month growing to $500-$2,000/month by year end.

The Combined Picture
Month 1: $7,600 (mostly skin sales)
Month 2: $2,490 (recurring building)
Month 3: $4,500
Month 4: $7,800
4-month cumulative: ~$22,390
These projections assume 500 DAU by month 1 and 2,000 DAU by month 4. The model does not depend on a single launch spike. It builds recurring revenue that compounds with player growth.

Unit Economics
Cost to acquire one player: $0 (organic growth, no paid ads)
Revenue per paying player: $1-$9
Revenue per sponsor: $200-$3,000
Protocol fee per match: 10% of prize pool (automatic)
Creator royalty per secondary trade: 5% (automatic, permanent)
The model is capital efficient by necessity. Built by one person with no budget.

13. RISK FACTORS
Every project that publishes a whitepaper without a risk section is either hiding something or has not thought carefully enough about what could go wrong.

Risk 1 -- Smart Contract Vulnerability: The V1 settlement contracts are unaudited. An undiscovered vulnerability could allow an attacker to drain prize funds.
Severity: High | Likelihood: Low-Medium
Mitigation: Prize pool caps enforced during beta. Contracts are intentionally minimal (smaller surface = smaller risk). Audit scheduled for month 3. Bridge architecture (WONs2) separates real funds from game operations.

Risk 2 -- Relay Server Centralisation: The V1 relay server is a single point of failure. If it goes down, matches cannot run.
Severity: Medium | Likelihood: Low (Cloud Run auto-scaling)
Mitigation: Multiple instances in parallel. Signed result architecture -- relay signs results, contract verifies signature. WONs2 decentralisation removes this risk entirely.

Risk 3 -- Player Retention Below Target: The 2,000 DAU target assumes the core loop retains players. If retention is lower, revenue targets are not met.
Severity: High | Likelihood: Medium
Mitigation: Referral system (referred players retain better). Economic retention layer (skin ownership, XP accumulation, micro-rewards). Milestone structure bounds downside.

Risk 4 -- Regulatory Exposure: Prize pool mechanics may attract scrutiny. Entry fees may be classified as gambling. Token sales may be classified as securities.
Severity: High if triggered | Likelihood: Low with correct structuring
Mitigation: Token launches only after legal opinion obtained. Prize pools structured as skill competitions, not gambling. No financial return promises. Delaware C-Corp established.

Risk 5 -- Key Person Dependency: The entire technical stack was built by one person.
Severity: High in WONs1, decreasing through WONs2 | Likelihood: Low but non-zero
Mitigation: Smart contracts are immutable -- settlements continue without founder. Two hires in month 2 ensure operations continuity. WONs2 open source makes the relay replaceable.

Risk 6 -- Chain Dependency: World of Nads currently deploys on Monad and targets Solana. Network outage or ecosystem shift would impact game availability.
Severity: Medium | Likelihood: Low
Mitigation: The multi-chain architecture provides redundancy. Relay and frontend layers are chain-agnostic. Only settlement contracts are chain-specific. Migration path exists.

Risk 7 -- Sponsor Pipeline Concentration: If B2B sponsor revenue concentrates in a few large sponsors and one cancels, revenue drops.
Severity: Medium | Likelihood: Medium in early months
Mitigation: Streamer economy runs in parallel from month 2. Battle pass provides recurring baseline revenue. Organic growth reduces sponsor dependency.

Overall Risk Assessment
All risks in this table are partially mitigated in WONs1. Full mitigation requires WONs2 decentralization, audit completion, and a team that is no longer one person deep. A project that claims zero risk has not looked hard enough.
